  <section class="section architecture-section" id="architecture">
    <div class="container">
      <div class="section-label">Architecture</div>
      <h2 class="section-title fade-in">Wonders Carved in Stone</h2>
      <p class="section-desc fade-in">The fort is a treasure trove of ancient architecture, natural formations, and spiritual spaces that have endured for over a millennium.</p>
      <div class="arch-grid">
        <div class="arch-card fade-in">
          <div class="arch-icon">🏔</div>
          <h3>Konkan Kada</h3>
          <p>The crown jewel of Harishchandragad — a massive concave cliff that plunges thousands of feet into the Konkan valley below. The overhang creates a dramatic reverse waterfall during monsoons, where water is blown upward by powerful winds.</p>
          <div class="arch-tag">Natural Formation</div>
        </div>
        <div class="arch-card fade-in">
          <div class="arch-icon">🛕</div>
          <h3>Harishchandreshwar Temple</h3>
          <p>An ancient Hemadpanthi-style temple dedicated to Lord Harishchandreshwar (Shiva), adorned with intricate stone carvings. The temple architecture reflects the sophisticated craftsmanship of early medieval Maharashtra, with sculpted panels depicting mythological scenes.</p>
          <div class="arch-tag">6th Century Temple</div>
        </div>
        <div class="arch-card fade-in">
          <div class="arch-icon">🌊</div>
          <h3>Kedareshwar Cave</h3>
          <p>A mystical cave housing a massive Shivling that stands 5 feet tall, perpetually surrounded by icy cold knee-deep water. The cave features four pillars, three of which have collapsed, which are said to symbolise the four yugas (epochs) in Hindu cosmology.</p>
          <div class="arch-tag">Sacred Cave</div>
        </div>
        <div class="arch-card fade-in">
          <div class="arch-icon">⛏</div>
          <h3>Rock-Cut Caves</h3>
          <p>Multiple rock-cut caves dot the fort complex, serving as ancient shelters, meditation spaces, and watchtowers. These caves showcase the engineering expertise of the fort's early inhabitants who carved directly into the basalt rock of the Sahyadris.</p>
          <div class="arch-tag">Ancient Caves</div>
        </div>
        <div class="arch-card fade-in">
          <div class="arch-icon">📐</div>
          <h3>Fort Elevation</h3>
          <p>Standing at approximately 4670 feet (1424 meters) above sea level, Harishchandragad commands spectacular panoramic views of the surrounding Sahyadri ranges, valleys, and on clear days, distant plains stretching towards the horizon.</p>
          <div class="arch-tag">4670 Feet High</div>
        </div>
        <div class="arch-card fade-in">
          <div class="arch-icon">🌿</div>
          <h3>Trek Pathways</h3>
          <p>Ancient carved steps and pathways wind through the fort complex, connecting its various structures. These stone paths were engineered to manage heavy monsoon rainfall and provide access even during the wettest seasons of the year.</p>
          <div class="arch-tag">Ancient Pathways</div>
        </div>
      </div>

  <section class="section facts-section" id="facts">
    <div class="container">
      <div class="section-label">Fascinating Facts</div>
      <h2 class="section-title fade-in">Did You Know?</h2>
      <div class="facts-grid">
        <div class="fact-card fade-in">
          <div class="fact-number">01</div>
          <div class="fact-icon">🏆</div>
          <h3>Most Popular Trek Fort</h3>
          <p>Harishchandragad is consistently ranked among the top 5 most popular trekking destinations in all of Maharashtra, attracting tens of thousands of trekkers annually from across India and abroad.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="fact-card fade-in">
          <div class="fact-number">02</div>
          <div class="fact-icon">💧</div>
          <h3>Reverse Waterfall</h3>
          <p>During monsoon season, powerful updrafts at Konkan Kada create a stunning natural phenomenon — water falls upward! The reverse waterfall occurs when strong Konkan winds blow water back up the cliff face.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="fact-card fade-in">
          <div class="fact-number">03</div>
          <div class="fact-icon">🛕</div>
          <h3>15-Century-Old Temples</h3>
          <p>The Harishchandreshwar and Kedareshwar temples are over 1,500 years old and still stand as remarkable examples of early medieval Maharashtrian temple architecture with intricate sculptural programs.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="fact-card fade-in">
          <div class="fact-number">04</div>
          <div class="fact-icon">🌄</div>
          <h3>360° Valley Views</h3>
          <p>From the summit plateau, trekkers can enjoy unobstructed 360-degree panoramic views covering multiple districts of Maharashtra, including sights of the Malshej Ghat, Bhandardara, and on clear days, distant plains.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="fact-card fade-in">
          <div class="fact-number">05</div>
          <div class="fact-icon">❄</div>
          <h3>Icy Sacred Waters</h3>
          <p>The water inside Kedareshwar Cave remains icy cold throughout the year, even in peak summer. The Shivling inside is one of the largest naturally-occurring lingams in the Sahyadri range.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="fact-card fade-in">
          <div class="fact-number">06</div>
          <div class="fact-icon">🦅</div>
          <h3>Rich Biodiversity</h3>
          <p>The forests around Harishchandragad are home to rich biodiversity including rare birds, endemic plant species of the Western Ghats UNESCO World Heritage Site, and various wildlife species.</p>

  <section class="section visit-section" id="visit">
    <div class="container">
      <div class="section-label">Plan Your Visit</div>
      <h2 class="section-title fade-in">How to Reach & Travel Tips</h2>
      <div class="visit-grid">
        <div class="visit-card fade-in">
          <div class="visit-card-header">
            <span class="visit-icon">🚌</span>
            <h3>How to Reach</h3>
          </div>
          <ul class="visit-list">
            <li><strong>By Road:</strong> Drive to Khireshwar or Pachnai village via Pune–Ahmednagar highway. Both villages are the base camps.</li>
            <li><strong>From Pune:</strong> ~120 km via Alephata. Take a bus to Alephata then a local auto/jeep to the base.</li>
            <li><strong>From Mumbai:</strong> ~180 km via Kalyan–Malshej route. Buses available to Khireshwar.</li>
            <li><strong>Nearest Town:</strong> Alephata (32 km) — last major stop for supplies.</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
        <div class="visit-card fade-in">
          <div class="visit-card-header">
            <span class="visit-icon">💡</span>
            <h3>Travel Tips</h3>
          </div>
          <ul class="visit-list">
            <li>Start your trek early morning (5–6 AM) to avoid afternoon heat and complete the round trip before sunset.</li>
            <li>Carry at least 3–4 litres of water per person. Water refills are available at Pachnai village.</li>
            <li>Pack high-energy snacks, a torch/headlamp, and a basic first-aid kit for the journey.</li>
            <li>Monsoon visits (Jul–Sep) are stunning but trails become slippery — take extra precautions.</li>
            <li>Camping is a wonderful experience; obtain permissions and carry a sleeping bag.</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
        <div class="visit-card fade-in">
          <div class="visit-card-header">
            <span class="visit-icon">⚠️</span>
            <h3>Safety Instructions</h3>
          </div>
          <ul class="visit-list">
            <li>Never approach the edge of Konkan Kada. The cliff is extremely dangerous with unpredictable winds.</li>
            <li>Do not attempt Nalichi Vaat without experienced guides and proper climbing equipment.</li>
            <li>Inform someone at the base village about your trekking plan and expected return time.</li>
            <li>Avoid solo trekking, especially on lesser-known trails or during monsoon season.</li>
            <li>Keep emergency contacts ready: local forest department and nearest hospital in Akola.</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
      </div>
